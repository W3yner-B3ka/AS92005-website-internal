Craters

The Moon's surface is scarred by billions of years of meteorite impacts. Without weather or tectonic activity to erode them, these craters are preserved exactly as they formed — some stretching hundreds of kilometres across.

300K+ craters wider than 1km
02

Water Ice
At Poles

Permanently shadowed craters near the lunar poles trap water ice, untouched by sunlight for billions of years. Sitting at temperatures as low as −240°C, these cold pockets are among the coldest places in the solar system — and one of the most promising resources for any future lunar base.

−240°C coldest points on the Moon
03

Potential
Resources

The Moon holds a wealth of materials critical for future space exploration. Polar water ice could be split into hydrogen fuel and breathable oxygen. The regolith is rich in helium-3, a rare isotope with potential for nuclear fusion. Titanium, aluminium, and silicon are abundant across the surface — raw materials for building.
